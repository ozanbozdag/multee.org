All measurements (CSV)

One row per sample, one column per kind of measurement. A sample is a population, isolate or strain at one timepoint; its code gives treatment, line and timepoint (PA2_t600_iso1 is isolate 1 of line PA2 at transfer 600). Choose columns under Trait columns; each trait is split into columns that are comparable down the page (same statistic, similar growth time), and one-off measurements sit in that trait’s Other column.

Hover over or click any value for how it was measured and where it was published. Values marked ≈ were read off a figure, so treat them as approximate. A dot means the sample was not measured for that column, which is not the same as “no”. Strains hides engineered strains by default.

Sample and strain codes match the Genome sequences table. To ask about measurements not listed here, use the request form. No MuLTEE transcriptome or proteome data are public yet.

Trait guide

What each trait records

Macroscopic
Whether clusters reach a size visible to the naked eye, as each paper judges it. Bozdag et al. 2023 calls clusters macroscopic when they are visible by eye (millimetre scale); Day et al. 2024 uses “larger than 1 mm”. Tong et al. 2025 gives no numeric threshold. The threshold a row uses is in its notes.
Entanglement
Whether branches from different parts of a cluster are physically interlocked, so the cluster holds together after mother–daughter bonds break. Each row gives the paper’s evidence: overlapping components in reconstructed clusters (Bozdag et al. 2023), the fraction of entangled clusters in a growth assay, or a jump in group size in engineered strains (Day et al. 2024).
Cluster size
Papers report different statistics, and they are not interchangeable: mean radius (Bozdag et al. 2021, Pineau et al. 2024), biomass-weighted mean radius (Bozdag et al. 2023, Tong et al. 2025), or the diameter of clusters squashed into modules under a coverslip (Yoon et al. 2025). The statistic and the growth time are given in every row.
Cell aspect ratio
Long axis over short axis of individual cells, usually from calcofluor-stained cells of crushed clusters.
Cell size
Mostly cell volume in µm³, computed from the long and short axes; the statistic says when another size measure is used.
Cell packing fraction
Fraction of a cluster’s volume occupied by cells.
Budding pattern
Where and when cells bud: bud angle relative to the mother’s axis, the timing of first and later divisions, and whether daughters divide in step with their mothers (mainly Cedeño-Pérez et al. 2025; also Day et al. 2024).
Ploidy
Genome copy number, from propidium iodide DNA-content peaks or from sequencing karyotypes (Tong et al. 2025). The t50 and t100 population values are computed from the paper’s cell-level data: the main DNA-content peak and the fraction of cells below 3N.
Mechanical properties
Toughness, fracture stress, compressive stress at a given strain, and single-cell stiffness (Bozdag et al. 2023), each with its unit.
Fluid flow through clusters
Whether fermentation-driven flows move fluid through the cluster interior (Narayanasamy et al. 2025).
Growth rate
Doublings per daily transfer (Bozdag et al. 2023), single-cell doubling time (Cedeño-Pérez et al. 2025), or the mode of growth of a single cluster over time (Narayanasamy et al. 2025).
Relative fitness
Competitive fitness against a labelled reference, mostly as a selection rate constant per day. The statistic names the competitor and the conditions give the assay environment, because values from different assays are not comparable.
Reproduction and propagules
Only direct measurements of how clusters reproduce, such as the fold increase in cluster number over a growth cycle. Model predictions are not included.
Coexisting types
Whether distinct size types (Small and Large) coexist within one population (Pineau et al. 2024). The frequency of the Large type is listed under Other measurements.
Other measurements
Strain-level phenotypes that fit none of the above, for example oxygen penetration depth, bud-scar size, chaperone levels, and the frequency of the Large type in a population.
